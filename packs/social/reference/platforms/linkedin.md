# LinkedIn

## What this feed rewards

Dwell time is the leading ranking signal in 2026: how long a viewer actually stays on a post outweighs likes or quick reactions, and posts holding attention past roughly 60 seconds get the strongest distribution. Document posts, PDF carousels, are currently the highest engagement native format on the platform, often running well ahead of plain text posts. Native video and first person, specific stories, a real number, a real decision, a real mistake, outperform generic advice posts and reshared articles. Genuine comment conversation, not just reaction counts, extends a post's life in the feed; the algorithm re-surfaces posts that keep generating real replies hours after posting.

## Formats

Text or image post: a single image at 4:5 (1080x1350) or 1:1 (1080x1080) both render well in feed.

Document post (carousel): the standout format right now; PDF slides, portrait orientation, roughly 6 to 10 slides for a finishable read.

Native video: 4:5 or 1:1 for feed autoplay, 9:16 works for anything framed as a short clip; avoid uploading raw 16:9 talking head footage, which gets pillarboxed.

The framework's compositor exports 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 from one generated scene. Default to 4:5 for standard posts, and route carousel style ideas to the document or PDF format, since that is currently the strongest performer.

## Copy norms

The feed truncates behind "see more" at roughly 200 to 220 characters on desktop and closer to 140 to 150 on mobile; the opening line has to work as a complete hook on its own, before the click. Sweet spot for full post length is 1,300 to 1,900 characters; short one liners and sprawling 3,000 character essays both underperform the middle.

Structure: hook line, then short paragraphs of one to two sentences each, with white space between them. LinkedIn's format rewards scannability over dense paragraphs. Emoji: sparing, mostly as bullet substitutes at the start of a line, not decorative.

Hashtags: 3 to 5, specific rather than broad, placed at the end. More than 5 has been shown to trigger a sharp reach reduction, current estimates put it around two thirds.

Links: a link in the post body still costs some reach relative to a native post with no link. The common workaround is posting natively and adding the link in the first comment, though a direct link in the body is not the reach killer it was once framed as. Verify current behavior in your own account before relying on either pattern.

## What gets punished

Engagement bait ("agree?", "comment YES") and automation pod style engagement, the same small ring of accounts liking and commenting on each other's posts within minutes, are both explicitly targeted by the platform's authenticity detection and get suppressed. Automated or templated looking comments read as inauthentic and damage the commenting account's own reach, not just the post's. Reposting an article or someone else's content with no original take underperforms a first person post by a wide margin. More than roughly one post a day starts cannibalizing its own reach; the feed punishes over-posting from the same profile more visibly than most platforms.

## Automation posture

Scheduling your own posts, whether through LinkedIn's native scheduler or an approved API integration, is normal, supported and unremarkable. Automating engagement is a different matter entirely: the platform's terms explicitly ban software that automates liking, commenting, sharing, connection requests or messaging, and a 2026 authenticity update specifically targets engagement pods and automated comment tools. Detection triggers reach suppression first, account restriction if it continues.

Safe to automate: publishing on schedule. Never automate: comments, connection requests, or DMs. Those stay human, every time, on this platform specifically.

## Cadence default

3 to 5 posts a week is a sane default; daily is workable only with genuinely daily worthy material, since posting twice in one day reliably splits the first post's reach.

Best windows: weekday mornings, roughly 8 to 10am in the audience's working timezone, catch the highest professional scroll activity; Tuesday through Thursday outperform Monday and Friday for most audiences. Verify against your own post analytics.

## The stop test here

A scroll stopping LinkedIn post opens with a line that reads as a complete thought on its own, before the "see more" click, usually a specific number, a contrarian claim, or the start of a story with real stakes. If the hook needs a second sentence to make sense, dwell time drops before it ever gets going.
