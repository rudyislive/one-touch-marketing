# Facebook

## What this feed rewards

Reels are the primary discovery surface again; short form video is how a page reaches beyond its existing followers, and skipping it forfeits most available organic reach. Original content ranks: Meta explicitly deprioritizes low value reposts, reaction clips with no commentary, and stitched or recycled material across both Feed and Reels. Outbound links no longer carry the heavy penalty they did through 2024 and 2025; referral traffic to external sites climbed sharply through 2026 as the "leak" suppression eased. A link is not the reach killer it used to be, though native video still wins on raw distribution. Comments that spark genuine back and forth outweigh passive likes, and Groups remain a distribution layer separate from the Page feed, reaching people a Page post never will.

## Formats

Feed image: 4:5 (1080x1350) is default for mobile screen coverage; 1:1 (1080x1080) and 1.91:1 (1200x628) link preview format both still work.

Feed video: 4:5 vertical or 1:1 square outperform 16:9 landscape on mobile; 1:1 alone claims meaningfully more in feed screen space.

Reels: 9:16 (1080x1920), same spec as Instagram and TikTok, and increasingly where the reach actually lives.

The framework's compositor exports 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 from one generated scene. Default to 4:5 for feed stills, 9:16 for anything routed to Reels.

## Copy norms

Caption length is forgiving, but lead with the hook in the first line since Facebook truncates after roughly two lines on mobile; let the rest run only if it earns the read. Register is plainer than Instagram: fewer emoji, more direct sentences, comments section tone rather than caption as performance.

Hashtags do almost nothing for reach here; a couple at most, only if genuinely a discovery term, never a block of ten. Links in caption are fine again; a clean single link with a stated reason to click outperforms burying it in a comment, though a bare link post with no framing still underperforms native video or image.

## What gets punished

Engagement bait, and reaction clip or stitch content with no original commentary, get algorithmically deprioritized on sight. Watermarked reposts from other platforms read as low effort and rank behind original captures. Posting the same asset across many Groups in a short window reads as spam behavior and risks a Group level or Page level restriction. A caption that is nothing but a link with no framing, or one buried under a wall of hashtags, both underperform.

## Automation posture

Scheduling Page posts, Reels and images through the Graph API, or Meta Business Suite's native scheduler, is standard, supported, and the normal way pages operate. Automated comment moderation (hiding or flagging toxic replies through Meta's own filters) is supported and safe. Automated commenting or DMing as the page, at volume, sits behind separate permissions reviewed individually and gets flagged fast if it looks scripted.

Safe to automate: publishing to Page and Reels. Needs a human hand: Group posting, since Groups read automation as spam faster than the Page feed does, and any comment or DM engagement beyond moderation.

## Cadence default

1 to 2 feed posts a day is the practical ceiling before reach per post starts declining; 3 to 5 Reels a week is a reasonable default alongside it.

Best windows: late morning through early afternoon on weekdays (the lunch break scroll) and early evening; the audience skews slightly older than Instagram's, so weekday daytime often outperforms late night. Verify against Page Insights.

## The stop test here

A scroll stopping Facebook post reads like something a friend would forward into a group chat: a clear claim or a relatable moment in the first line, one image or a short video that makes its point without sound, and a comment worth replying to. If it only works as an announcement, it stops no one.
