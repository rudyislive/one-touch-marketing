# Pinterest

## What this feed rewards

Pinterest ranks for relevance and save or click intent, not follower count or recency alone; a two year old pin can still surface today if it matches what someone is searching for right now. Fresh Pins, a new image against a URL, including a new design variation of existing content, get an early visibility boost, especially when they pick up saves or clicks quickly after publishing. The platform functions like a visual search engine: keyword rich titles, descriptions and board names drive discovery the way search engine optimization does, more than any single algorithm trick. Video content and native Pinterest Shop integration are getting increased placement priority through 2026; a static pin now competes against video pins for the same slots.

## Formats

Standard Pin: 2:3 (1000x1500) is the canonical, best performing ratio; anything taller than roughly 1:2.1 gets cropped in feed and loses its bottom content.

Video Pin: 9:16 (1080x1920), 3 to 60 seconds per page, up to 20 pages. This format absorbed what used to be called Idea Pins, which Pinterest folded into the unified Pin format; Idea Pins now survive only as a paid ad type, not an organic one.

Square (1:1) works but concedes feed real estate to 2:3; use it only when the source asset genuinely does not crop well taller.

The framework's compositor exports 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 from one generated scene. For Pinterest, treat 4:5 as the floor and prefer cropping or generating toward the platform's true 2:3 native ratio where the pipeline allows it, since 2:3 measurably outperforms squarer crops here.

## Copy norms

Title: keyword led, natural language, not keyword stuffed; put the specific search term a person would actually type early in the title. Description: 100 to 200 characters, one or two key phrases woven in naturally, written like a helpful caption rather than a tag cloud.

Hashtags contribute little to discovery compared to title and description keywords; a couple of specific ones do no harm, but they are not the primary lever the way they are elsewhere.

Links: Pinterest is the one major platform where the outbound link is the entire point. Every pin should lead somewhere; a pin with no destination underperforms one that has one, and the platform actively favors links to real, working landing pages over generic homepage links.

## What gets punished

Text heavy overlays that are hard to read at thumbnail size get skipped in the scroll; the image has to work small before it works large. Reused, unmodified pins with no fresh variation stop earning the Fresh Pin boost and plateau in reach. Broken or slow loading destination links get demoted, since the platform optimizes for the full journey from pin to landing page, not just the click. Aggressive keyword stuffing in titles or descriptions reads as spam to the ranking system, the same way it would to a search engine.

## Automation posture

Publishing pins on a schedule through the Pinterest API is standard, supported, and requires only a Business account; most schedulers handle it end to end, including mixed image and video pins. There is no meaningful organic engagement automation risk here the way there is on LinkedIn or Instagram, since Pinterest has no comment reply or DM culture driving reach. The main risk is volume based, publishing so many near duplicate pins so fast that it reads as spam rather than a real content library.

Safe to automate: the entire publish step, at scale, including batch scheduling many fresh variations of one asset over time.

## Cadence default

1 to 2 fresh pins a day is a sane steady default for a single active board strategy; multiple boards can each carry their own fresh pin without it reading as spam, since it is a different context each time.

Best windows: evenings and weekends tend to outperform weekday work hours, since usage skews toward planning and browsing time rather than workday scrolling. Verify against your own analytics.

## The stop test here

A scroll stopping pin reads clearly at thumbnail size from an arm's length away: one bold visual idea, a short readable text overlay if any, and a destination worth the click. If it only makes sense zoomed in, it will not survive the grid.
