# Instagram

## What this feed rewards

Shares to DMs outrank likes as a ranking signal by a wide margin, so build things a stranger forwards to one specific friend, not just double taps. Saves are the second currency: frameworks, comparisons, step by steps, anything worth returning to. For Reels specifically, average watch through and replay rate outrank raw view count; a tight 12 second Reel watched to the end beats a rambling 45 second one people bail on at 3 seconds. Instagram's originality ranking actively deprioritizes recycled or aggregator content and detects rival platform watermarks (TikTok/CapCut burn ins), throttling them on sight. Carousels reward slow scroll and repeat visits; up to 20 slides are supported, though 6 to 10 is the sweet spot for finishing rate.

## Formats

Feed image or carousel: 4:5 (1080x1350) is default, it fills the most mobile screen. 1:1 (1080x1080) is acceptable and crops less on shares. Carousels run 4:5 per slide, 6 to 10 slides typical, 20 max.

Reels and Stories: 9:16 (1080x1920), full screen. Reels are the platform's primary discovery surface now; anything not shot for 9:16 gets letterboxed and reads as an afterthought.

The framework's compositor exports 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 from one generated scene. Default to 4:5 for feed and carousel work, 9:16 for anything routed to Reels or Stories.

## Copy norms

Front load the first 125 characters; that is what shows before "more" on mobile. Sweet spot for a feed caption is 125 to 150 characters, unless the post is built for saves (education, storytelling), where longer runs fine up to the 2,200 character ceiling. Hook line first, always. Value in the middle, one clear ask at the end.

Emoji: light and functional, breaking up a list or marking a beat, never a costume for weak copy. Hashtags: 3 to 5, specific to the niche, placed at the end of the caption or in the first comment; a broad tag block reads as spam and the platform has effectively capped how many do any work anyway.

Links do not work in caption text; the bio link or the native link sticker on Stories is the only route. Write the CTA around the bio, not around a pasted URL.

## What gets punished

Engagement bait ("comment YES if", "tag someone who") gets suppressed on sight. Recycled content, reposted screenshots with no commentary, and rival platform watermarks all get demoted under the originality ranking. Posting to feed more than once or twice a day trains the algorithm to read you as noisy rather than caring, and often costs reach on the very next post. A caption that reads like it was pasted from another platform, no native hook, generic phrasing, underperforms even with a strong image.

## Automation posture

Scheduling stills, carousels, Reels and Stories through the Instagram Graph API is normal and fully supported; most schedulers cover it end to end. Automated comment replies and DM automation sit behind separate, narrower permissions reviewed individually, and unreviewed automated engagement (auto liking, auto following, mass auto commenting) gets accounts throttled or suspended.

Safe to automate: publishing. Needs review before automating: anything touching another user's DMs or comments at volume.

## Cadence default

3 to 4 Reels a week, 2 to 3 carousels, 1 to 2 static posts; one feed post a day is a sane ceiling, Stories can run more freely since they don't compete for the same ranking slot.

Best windows: late morning (the coffee scroll) and early evening (commute home, post dinner wind down) on weekdays; weekend mornings also perform well. Verify against your own account's audience insights.

## The stop test here

A scroll stopping Instagram post is a first frame that reads as a moment already in progress, not an announcement, paired with a hook that promises a payoff worth three more seconds. If the first frame could be a stock photo, it fails.
